Why the progress ring & shards work — completion bias
A partially-filled progress bar is one of the strongest motivational signals in behavioural psychology. The brain treats incomplete progress as an open loop it wants to close — the same mechanism that makes you finish a season of a show. Each compass shard converts an abstract "do tasks" goal into a visible, almost-complete pattern. Once you're at 6/8 shards, skipping today no longer feels neutral — it feels like leaving something unfinished.
Why specific number goals work — Locke & Latham
Edwin Locke and Gary Latham spent thirty years showing that specific, difficult goals consistently produce higher performance than vague "do your best" intentions — across roughly 400 studies. "Read 12 books" beats "read more" because the brain can measure the gap, picture the finish line, and track the rate. The progress bars on each goal exploit the same principle: they convert distance-to-target into a single visible number, which is the form your brain prefers to work with.
Why streaks work — the loss aversion lever
Psychologists Tversky and Kahneman showed that people feel losses roughly twice as strongly as equivalent gains. A streak turns daily action into a number you'd be reluctant to lose — every day completed is built-up value, and breaking it now feels like throwing something away. That's why a 30-day streak is far more motivating than a "do this thirty times" checklist, even though they're identical. The longer your streak runs, the harder it gets to skip a day.

Everything is stored locally in your browser (using localStorage) on the device you're using. Areviax OS does not have a server and does not transmit your data anywhere automatically.

If you subscribe to Areviax OS, your payment is processed by our payment provider, not stored or seen by us directly beyond what's needed to manage your subscription. We do not store your full card details.
